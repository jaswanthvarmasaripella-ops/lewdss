Alert Center & NDMA Response Protocols

Institutional four-tier hazard categorization mapped to National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) Standard Operating Procedures.

Minimal Risk (RLSI < 0.25)

Normal background environmental state. Stable hillside regolith with routine moisture drainage.

  • Maintain standard routine monitoring of community rain gauges.
  • Ensure hillside roadside ditches and cross-culverts remain clear of debris.
  • Standard transport movement permitted along all mountain highways.

Advisory (0.25 ≤ RLSI < τ*)

Elevated antecedent moisture. Soil pore pressures beginning to increase in convergent hollows.

  • Alert Gram Panchayat disaster scouts in vulnerable escarpment hamlets.
  • Conduct precautionary inspections of active road-cutting toe slopes.
  • District Emergency Operations Centre (DEOC) initiates 6-hour rainfall polling.

Watch Warning (τ* ≤ RLSI < 0.65)

Operational threshold breached. Subterranean shear stress approaching critical failure envelope.

  • Pre-position debris clearing equipment along vulnerable National Highway sectors.
  • Issue advisories against non-essential night travel through active ghat sections.
  • State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) units placed on standby readiness.

Severe Warning (RLSI ≥ 0.65)

Extreme saturation and intense rainfall triggering imminent slope failure hazard.

  • Execute immediate evacuation of high-risk settlement corridors to designated shelters.
  • Suspend vehicular traffic on identified unstable mountain pass corridors.
  • Mobilize National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) rescue teams to forward bases.

Model Diagnostics & Spatial Cross-Validation Benchmark

Audited Leave-One-District-Out cross-validation comparing Balanced Logistic Regression against Random Forest across 8 spatial folds.

Spatial Holdout Evaluation: Logistic Regression vs Random Forest

Cross-district spatial validation tests true geographic transfer by holding out an entire administrative district during training. Balanced Logistic Regression demonstrates reliable generalizability with 87.0% overall recall across held-out districts. In contrast, Random Forest exhibits severe threshold collapse under spatial domain shift (e.g. 0% recall on Kodagu fold when using training thresholds), proving that uncalibrated tree models cannot safely transfer across mountain belts without local retuning.

Scientific Attribution Notice:

SHAP values explain statistical model attribution relative to regional training backgrounds. Positive SHAP values indicate that the feature shifted model output towards higher relative susceptibility; negative values indicate that the feature reduced model score. They represent correlation under empirical cross-validation, not deterministic physical geotechnical causation.

OPERATIONAL NOTICE: This regional decision support system outputs a statistical Relative Landslide Susceptibility Index (RLSI) ranking terrain-rainfall conditions against verified regional backgrounds. It is an engineering research aid and does not replace official meteorological advisories issued by the District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA) or National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA).
